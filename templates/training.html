Cybersecurity Training

Review the training topics below before completing the cybersecurity quizzes. These topics will help you recognise common cyber threats and use safer online practices.

1. Phishing Awareness

Phishing is a cyberattack where criminals use fake emails, messages or websites to trick people into revealing sensitive information such as passwords, banking details or personal information.

Warning signs can include unexpected requests for personal information, suspicious links, unusual attachments and messages that create urgency.

Avoid clicking suspicious links and verify unexpected requests before providing sensitive information.

2. Password & Account Security

Strong passwords help protect online accounts from unauthorised access. Passwords should be difficult to guess and should not contain easily identified personal information.

Different passwords should be used for different accounts. This limits the damage if one password becomes compromised.

A password manager can also help users create and manage different passwords securely.

3. Multi-Factor Authentication

Multi-factor authentication (MFA) adds another layer of security to an account by requiring additional verification as well as a password.

For example, a user may be asked to confirm a login using an authentication app or another verification method.

MFA can help protect an account even if its password has been compromised.

4. Social Engineering

Social engineering involves manipulating people into revealing information or performing unsafe actions.

Attackers may impersonate trusted people or organisations and use urgency, fear, curiosity or trust to influence a victim.

Unusual requests should be verified independently before sensitive information is provided or an action is taken.

5. Malware & Ransomware

Malware is malicious software designed to damage systems, steal information or interfere with normal computer operations.

Ransomware is a type of malware that can lock or encrypt data and demand payment from the victim.

Avoid suspicious downloads and attachments, and keep software updated to reduce the risk of malware infections.

6. Safe Browsing & Email Security

Websites, links, downloads and email attachments should be treated carefully, particularly when they are unexpected.

Check website addresses before entering sensitive information and verify unexpected messages or attachments before opening them.

Avoid downloading files from unknown or untrusted sources.

7. Online Privacy & Personal Data

Personal information shared online can be used by criminals for scams, impersonation and other attacks.

Users should consider what information they share publicly and review the privacy settings of their online accounts.

Sensitive information such as passwords, financial details and identification information should not be shared with unknown or unverified people.

8. Public Wi-Fi & Network Safety

Public Wi-Fi networks can present security risks because users may not know who operates the network or who else is connected to it.

Avoid performing sensitive activities on networks that you do not trust, particularly when entering passwords or financial information.

Check that you are connecting to the correct network and use trusted connections when handling sensitive information.

9. Device & Software Security

Keeping devices and software secure reduces the risk of attackers exploiting known security weaknesses.

Operating systems, applications and security software should be kept updated when security updates become available.

Devices should also be protected with appropriate access controls, such as a password, PIN or other secure locking method.
