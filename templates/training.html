Cybersecurity Training

Complete the training topics below before taking the cybersecurity quiz. These topics will help you recognise common cyber threats and use safer online practices.

1. Phishing Awareness

Phishing is a cyberattack where criminals use fake emails, messages or websites to trick people into revealing sensitive information such as passwords, banking details or personal information.

A common warning sign of phishing is an unexpected request for personal information. Phishing messages may also contain suspicious links, unexpected attachments or requests asking you to act quickly.

If you receive a suspicious email or link, do not click it or provide personal information. Ignore the message or report it through the appropriate security channel.

2. Password Security

Strong passwords help protect online accounts from unauthorised access. A strong password should be difficult to guess and can include uppercase and lowercase letters, numbers and special characters.

Avoid simple passwords such as "password123", common words, usernames or personal information that could be guessed easily.

You should also use different passwords for different accounts. If one password becomes compromised, using unique passwords limits the damage because attackers cannot use the same password to access all of your other accounts.

3. Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

Multi-factor authentication provides an additional layer of security when logging into an account.

Instead of relying only on a password, MFA requires another method of verification. For example, this could be a security code, authentication application or another verification method.

This makes an account more difficult for an attacker to access, even if the password has been compromised.

4. Safe Browsing and Email Attachments

Before entering sensitive information on a website, check the website address carefully and make sure it is legitimate. Cybercriminals may create fake websites that look similar to genuine websites in order to steal information.

You should also be careful with unexpected email attachments. Malicious attachments may contain harmful software.

If you receive an unexpected attachment, verify the sender and the attachment before opening it. Do not disable security software or open an attachment simply because it appears to come from someone you recognise.

5. Social Engineering

Social engineering is the manipulation of people into revealing information or performing unsafe actions.

Attackers may pretend to be trusted people or organisations and attempt to create urgency, fear or curiosity. Their aim may be to persuade someone to reveal a password, provide personal information, click a malicious link or open an unsafe attachment.

Always verify unusual or unexpected requests before providing sensitive information or taking an action.

6. Malware and Ransomware

Malware is malicious software designed to harm systems, steal information or interfere with normal computer operations.

Ransomware is a type of malware that can lock or encrypt data and demand payment from the victim.

To reduce the risk, avoid suspicious downloads and unexpected attachments, keep software updated and follow safe browsing practices.
